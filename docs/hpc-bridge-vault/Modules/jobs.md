# jobs.py

> [!abstract] Role
> Batch phase 2a: the budget envelope, Jobs mode (`submit_job`) and decision D2's gate and adopt — with the [[meter]] and the [[ledger]]'s `envelope` and `job` tables. The tools are in [[The MCP tools]]; the stop's half is in [[server]]. Design: [[Batch and swarm execution]] §2, §3, §12 D2.

## What it does

- **`submit_job`** (`submit_job` → `spec_of` → `find_envelope` → `_open_and_admit` / `_offer_again` / `_admit`): SSH facilities only. On a facility MEP it refuses with the reason (no free status or cancel channel; on ALCF Jobs mode collapses into Campaign). `spec_of` checks every argument before anything is written, because none may become shell code (rule 7): partition and account names, `walltime`, `array` (`0-19%4`: tasks, throttle), `depends_on` (job- ids in the ledger), `session_id` (Session's own check), and the size (`resources.requested`). It then reads the size and the rate:
  - `resources.charge` with the partition's billing weights;
  - otherwise the job's billed cores (Slurm's default billing), never a whole node for a slice;
  - a cost it cannot bound is refused.
  The worst case is tasks × rate × max(walltime, the queue's `min_charge_h`).
- **The envelope** (`EnvelopeInfo`).
  - **Opening.** With none open for this session on the facility and account, the first call answers `needs_confirmation`, reason `envelope_needed`, with the `terms` and the proposed envelope (cap = the request's, or the job's worst case rounded UP to 2 decimals; deadline 24 h by default, at most 168 h). Nothing is written. The confirming call opens it, after the operator ceiling (`HPC_BRIDGE_ENVELOPE_MAX`, summed over open envelopes on the same facility for EVERY account the envelope admits — `_ceiling_room`, then again inside `Ledger.add_envelope`'s transaction, `EnvelopeCeiling`) and the envelope's own dimensions (`max_jobs` counting array tasks, `max_nodes_per_block`, partitions).
  - **Rounding** (live 2026-11-01: "approve at least 3.33" for a worst case of 3.333…): needs (`_gu`, `terms.worst_case`, `JobInfo.worst_case`) are rounded up, what remains (`_gd`, `EnvelopeInfo.remaining`) down, caps are exact.
  - **Ownership.** It is `approved_by` this server's `AppCtx.session_token`. One whose session is gone (its owner's pid dead, as the batch lease is judged) is offered again (`envelope_new_session`, naming what remains); the yes re-binds it (`rebind_envelope`, the cap changed if the yes named one). One whose owner still runs is that session's, so this session proposes its own.
  - **Admission inside it.** A job that fits is admitted on the first call. One that doesn't answers `envelope_exhausted` (the state turns `exhausted`), naming what still bills and the unledgered jobs it counts. A yes with a larger `envelope.cap` raises the cap.
- **`_admit`, the order that holds rule 1:**
  1. a meter tick — the first job of an envelope is admitted only once the envelope has been read (`metered_at`); an unreadable accounting then refuses it (`meter_unread`, review B3);
  2. `Ledger.reserve_job` (one `BEGIN IMMEDIATE`: the envelope admits, the job passes its dimensions, the hold plus its worst case is within the cap);
  3. `write_job_script` (with the job id in `AppCtx.jobs_admitting` from here);
  4. `Ledger.move_job(only_from=("reserved",), unless_cancel=True)` — a stop or cancel that set `cancel_requested` meanwhile wins and the job is never submitted (review B2);
  5. `_submit_script`; a `cancel_requested` that came while the submit was in flight cancels it at once.

  A refused submit releases the reservation (`refused`), quoting the scheduler. A submit whose answer never came — or that exited 0 printing no job id (review A8) — is `submit_unknown`: it keeps the reservation, the meter's sweep finds it by its name `hpcb-<job id>` in the accounting, a stop cancels it by that name (`scancel --name=` / `qselect -N | qdel`), and it is never submitted again. A `reserved` row no admission finished, that the accounting never lists after `STALE_RESERVATION_S`, becomes `refused`.
- **The frozen job.sh** (`job_script`, `write_job_script(app, job_id, text, session_id=, deadline=)`): `<scratch>/jobs/<job id>/job.sh`, written once under noclobber (an existing one is refused, never replaced), and its sha256 read on the facility must equal the one meant.
  - **Header.** Built from the terms. Slurm: `--job-name=hpcb-<job id>`, nodes, time, `--deadline=<the cluster's local time>`, partition, account, `--exclusive` or `--ntasks-per-node=1 --cpus-per-task` (+`--mem`, `--gres`), `--array`, `--dependency`. The deadline is `DEADLINE_MARK` in the text; the writing call says it in the facility's local time (`date -d @N`, else BSD `date -r N`) and the hash is checked against the text with that value — sbatch reads `--deadline` in the submitting shell's time zone, and a TZ forced on sbatch would reach the job (probe, 2026-11-01). PBS: `-N`, `select`, walltime, `place=excl`, `-q`, `-A`, `-J`, `-W depend`.
  - **Body.** The session's `.cwd` and `.env` as they were at submit — copied beside the script (`session.cwd`, `session.env`, read-only) by the writing call (review A7: the job used to read them when it RAN) — then the command carried base64 (so no line of it is a directive), then `exit_code[.<task>]` written beside the script.
  - **Submission.** Slurm runs `sbatch --parsable --output=<dir>/slurm-%j.out` (`%A_%a` for arrays); PBS runs `qsub -o <dir>/pbs.out -j oe`. The receipt is read by `_receipts_in`.
- **The meter's tick** (`tick`): one login-shape call per tick over the facility's envelopes ([[meter]]). It updates every job row's `charged`, `committed`, `meter_source`, state and array counts, and each envelope's spent, committed and the unledgered jobs it counts (less `excluded` and the ids in `also_known`, jobs being adopted right now; on the envelope's accounts; one with no time limit to the deadline). An unread accounting keeps the last unledgered count (review B3) and does not set `metered_at`; an ended job it does not list keeps its last reading. A job still listed by squeue after the accounting says it ended is `running` (draining, review B6). It also applies the envelope's deadline (`at_deadline` `drain` or `abort`; an abort never cancels a job submitted unheld) and writes `meter_reading` rows and the `meter.tick` journal row.
- **`status` / `cancel` of `job-` and `env-` ids** answer in the one `BatchStatus` model (`kind`, `job`, `envelope`) and in `CancelResult`; both reconcile any open hand submit first. For a job, `abort` scancels or qdels it and `drain` cancels only its pending tasks. For an envelope, `drain` admits nothing more and `abort` cancels every job of it except one submitted unheld (named, with its own `cancel('job-…')`). `_scancel` cancels per id in one call and says, per id, whether the scheduler took it (`Cancelled`): the notice says `cancelled: <ids>` only for those (review B8), and names what it refused. A cancel says `draining` while one can still bill and `down` once none can.
- **D2 (c)** (`gate_envelope`, `gated_outcome`, `begin_hand`, `held_command`, `reconcile_hand`, `finish_hand`, `reconcile_pending`). The safety property: hpc-bridge never cancels, holds or releases a job it cannot prove is this command's or already ours.
  - **Gate.** A recognised `sbatch`/`qsub` ANYWHERE on the line (`Submission.names`: `srun …; sbatch x` was named by its srun, review A4) while this session's envelope is open answers `needs_confirmation` and runs nothing. salloc and srun keep #176's advisory. `server._login_shell` refuses such a command (a fresh SSH is outside the hold), and `server._run_batch` refuses a template that submits jobs, pointing at `submit_job(array=…)` (review A6).
  - **The hold** (`begin_hand`, before the dispatch is counted): one login call writes `<scratch>/hand/<token>/hpcb-shim/{sbatch|qsub}` (the wrapper, `_SHIM_SLURM` / `_SHIM_PBS`), an empty `mark`, and `before` — the user's jobs the scheduler lists now (`_snapshot`) — and the `hand` ledger row is written BEFORE the command runs. `held_command` puts the wrapper first on PATH with `HPCB_SHIM_*` set, runs the command (base64, its own `eval`), then takes both off again so the session's persisted env never carries them. A PATH wrapper, not a function: `command`, `env`, `nohup`, `timeout`, `nice`, `exec`, `xargs` reach it (review A3).
  - **The wrapper** runs the REAL sbatch/qsub with the original PATH and none of its variables (nothing reaches the job under `--export=ALL`), adding `--hold --comment=hpcb-<token> --deadline=now+<seconds to the envelope's deadline>` (PBS: `-h`) in the SAME submit — a user may not `scontrol update Deadline=` after (probe §1) — and appends the real command's own stdout (its receipt) to `mark`, with `HPCB_SHIM_START` / `HPCB_SHIM_RC=` around each call. `--wait` / `-W block=true` are never held; a hold the user asked for is left in place.
  - **Reconciliation** (`reconcile_hand`, on ANY outcome — the next status / submit_job / stop for a command still running or a server that died): one call reads `mark`, the scheduler's list now (`after`), and the request of every job new since `before` (`TZ=UTC0 scontrol show job -o`, `qstat -f -F json`). A job is the command's only by its receipt in `mark` (`_receipts_in`: never digits in its stdout, review A1; a PBS receipt's server starts with a letter). Then one meter tick (with those ids `also_known`), and per marked job: its request (`_slurm_request`: ReqTRES / AllocTRES / TRES billing, the most of them, stepped arrays by their step, review A5/B4; `_pbs_request`: `array_indices_submitted`), its worst case never below the queue's minimum (`_worst`), and `_hand_misfit` — Slurm's deadline read back and not later than the envelope's (review A4), the walltime able to finish before it. A fit is reserved as a `held` row and released; a misfit, or one over the cap, is cancelled while still held (it never ran). Releases and cancels go in ONE login call (`_release_and_cancel`), each answer parsed per id: a failed release stays held and is said; a failed cancel is ledgered as ours. A job new since the snapshot with no mark — and not held — is the wrapper's blind spot (an absolute path, sudo resetting PATH): reserved over the cap if need be (`unheld=1`), named "submitted unheld, no deadline applied", and NEVER cancelled by hpc-bridge on its own (only by `cancel('job-…')`, the user's say-so). The hand closes once the command has ended (or wrote `HPCB_HAND_DONE`) with no submit in flight, its envelope closes, or a day passes; the next `begin_hand` removes closed hands' directories. About five login calls per hand submit, however many jobs it makes (review A8).
  - **`submit_job(adopt=<id>)`** brings an existing job under the envelope, at its full request and never below the queue's minimum. It is never cancelled for not fitting (it already runs); the user is asked instead.
- **The compute block** (`block_admission(app, shape, partition=, account=, sizing=)`, called by `_ensure_endpoint_up` with `confirm_spend` before its lock): while this session's envelope is open, a block it confirms is reserved as a `pool_block` row at the size THAT call confirms — its nodes, partition, account, the partition's billing (else billed cores) × max(walltime, the queue's minimum charge) (review B1). A confirmed block resized by a later confirmation is reserved again. If it doesn't fit, the answer is `needs_confirmation` naming the envelope. The reservation holds until the stop (`close_admission`). The block's own scheduler job, once known (`ShapeRuntime.block_job`), is linked to the reservation (`_link_block`); every other parsl pilot still counts as unledgered.
- **The stop** (`billing_jobs`, `stop_question`, `cancel_for_stop`, `keep_for_stop`, `after_stop`, `stop_without_channel`, `close_admission`, `ledger_unreadable`; called by `server._stop_endpoint`):
  - **Which jobs.** Every job of ours that can still bill (any session's, a dead server's, a held one), on ANY facility (review B6): this facility's read fresh by the meter (open hand submits reconciled first) when a login channel is bound; every other's from the ledger, bounded by its deadline + walltime. Blocks are the stop's own.
  - **With no choice**, the stop asks and changes nothing.
  - **`jobs="cancel"`** cancels this facility's (a submit of unknown fate by name; an admission in flight asked to give up) and drains their envelope. A job submitted unheld, and another facility's, are not cancelled from here and stay listed. The answer is `draining` while one can still bill, then `down`.
  - **`jobs="keep"`** leaves them running under their envelope (now `draining`: status and cancel only) and their `--deadline`; an admission in flight gives up. The answer is `draining`, never `down`, and names them and their `cancel(…)`.
  - **With no endpoint bound**, nothing can be cancelled from here, and the answer says to connect first.
  - **An unreadable ledger** (`ledger_unreadable`): the blocks are released and the answer is never `down`.
  - **After any stop** the facility's envelopes admit nothing more.

> [!warning] The envelope binds only what hpc-bridge starts
> `terms.binds` says it and the envelope repeats it. A submit the lexer cannot see (Python's subprocess, an `sbatch` inside a script) passes the gate, and one that bypasses PATH (an absolute path, sudo) passes the hold; the meter's sweep counts and names them (the second also as an unheld adopted job). A stolen Compute token is not bound at all. The site backstop is "none" unless the user names one; on the fake cluster's `site` profile, `AccountingStorageEnforce=…,safe` makes Slurm hold a job that could pass its GrpTRESMins.
