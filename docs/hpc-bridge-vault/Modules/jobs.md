# jobs.py

> [!abstract] Role
> Batch phase 2a: the budget envelope, Jobs mode (`submit_job`) and decision D2's gate and adopt — with the [[meter]] and the [[ledger]]'s `envelope` and `job` tables. The tools are in [[The MCP tools]]; the stop's half is in [[server]]. Design: [[Batch and swarm execution]] §2, §3, §12 D2.

## What it does

- **`submit_job`** (`submit_job` → `spec_of` → `find_envelope` → `_open_and_admit` / `_offer_again` / `_admit`): SSH facilities only. On a facility MEP it refuses with the reason (no free status or cancel channel; on ALCF Jobs mode collapses into Campaign). `spec_of` checks every argument before anything is written, because none may become shell code (rule 7): partition and account names, `walltime`, `array` (`0-19%4`: tasks, throttle), `depends_on` (job- ids in the ledger), `session_id` (Session's own check), and the size (`resources.requested`). It then reads the size and the rate:
  - `resources.charge` with the partition's billing weights;
  - otherwise the job's billed cores (Slurm's default billing), never a whole node for a slice;
  - a cost it cannot bound is refused.
  The worst case is tasks × rate × max(walltime, the queue's `min_charge_h`).
- **The envelope** (`EnvelopeInfo`).
  - **Opening.** With none open for this session on the facility and account, the first call answers `needs_confirmation`, reason `envelope_needed`, with the `terms` and the proposed envelope (deadline 24 h by default, at most 168 h). Nothing is written. The proposal is made after a meter reading (`_held_now`: the jobs hpc-bridge did not start, as the meter counts them): its default cap covers what they already hold plus the job's worst case, rounded UP to 2 decimals, and it names them (`EnvelopeInfo.unledgered`, the notice, `exclude_jobs`). The session remembers the cap it proposed (`AppCtx.jobs_proposals`, `_proposal_key`): a yes with no cap of its own opens at that cap; when what is held outgrew it by the yes — or no proposal of this server preceded the yes (a restart, a skipped question) — it is proposed again (`envelope_needed`, saying why), never refused for an amount the user could not see nor opened at one they did not see (round 2; live: the proposal was the worst case alone, a leftover job counted by the yes, and the yes refused itself). A proposal is STABLE (round 3): it names an absolute deadline that the yes and every re-proposal reuse (the envelope opens with it), a held job with no time limit — or running past it — is held to that deadline (so its charged + committed does not grow with the user's think-time), and the cap carries a minute of the running held jobs' billing for the clock's jitter between two reads: four yeses cannot loop. A yes with its own cap that cannot hold what is held plus the job asks (`envelope_exhausted`, naming the jobs), opening nothing. The confirming call opens it, after the operator ceiling (`HPC_BRIDGE_ENVELOPE_MAX`, summed over open envelopes on the same facility for EVERY account the envelope admits — `_ceiling_room`, then again inside `Ledger.add_envelope`'s transaction, `EnvelopeCeiling`) and the envelope's own dimensions (`max_jobs` counting array tasks, `max_nodes_per_block`, partitions).
  - **Rounding** (live 2026-11-01: "approve at least 3.33" for a worst case of 3.333…): needs (`_gu`, `terms.worst_case`, `JobInfo.worst_case`) are rounded up, what remains (`_gd`, `EnvelopeInfo.remaining`) down, caps are exact.
  - **Ownership.** It is `approved_by` this server's `AppCtx.session_token`. One whose session is gone (its owner's pid dead, as the batch lease is judged) is offered again (`envelope_new_session`, naming what remains); the yes re-binds it (`rebind_envelope`, the cap changed if the yes named one). One whose owner still runs is that session's, so this session proposes its own.
  - **Admission inside it.** A job that fits is admitted on the first call. One that doesn't answers `envelope_exhausted` (the state turns `exhausted`), naming what still bills and the unledgered jobs it counts. A yes with a larger `envelope.cap` raises the cap.
  - **What still bills** (`_bills_note`): every job of the envelope that can still bill, and the unledgered jobs it counts — never one that ended with nothing committed (the demo of 2026-11-03 listed a CANCELLED job "+ 0"); what an ended job billed stays in `spent`.
  - **The session's own compute block** (`session_blocks`, `_mark_session_blocks`; the demo of 2026-11-03): a block confirmed BEFORE the envelope opened is not its reservation, so the meter's sweep counts it with the jobs hpc-bridge did not start. It stays counted (it bills the same allocation), but is named for what it is — `UnledgeredJob.session_block`, "the session's compute block, confirmed before this envelope opened" (`SESSION_BLOCK`) — and never offered to `exclude_jobs` (`_unledgered_note`, the proposal's held note). The session's blocks are every job a check of ours proved up on the bound endpoint (`AppCtx.job_seen_since`), each shape's confirmed or presumed block, and the blocks a stop or a switch left (`left_blocks`).
  - **Exit codes** (`job_info`): shown only for a job that ended COMPLETED or FAILED ([[meter]] `_EXIT_CODED`); a ledger an earlier build wrote may hold sacct's `0:0` for a live or cancelled job, and it reads null.
- **`_admit`, the order that holds rule 1:**
  1. a meter tick — the first job of an envelope is admitted only once the envelope has been read (`metered_at`); an unreadable accounting then refuses it (`meter_unread`, review B3). Not a busy channel: a scheduler command of hpc-bridge's own still running in the internal login session (one past its sync-wait) is waited for, up to `LOGIN_BUSY_WAIT_S` (30 s) per read (`_login_free`, through `batch.login_busy`), and if it still runs the answer is `busy` (`login_channel_busy`) — retryable, nothing submitted or refused, the envelope open with its yes (sweep 2026-11-02);
  2. `Ledger.reserve_job` (one `BEGIN IMMEDIATE`: the envelope admits, the job passes its dimensions, the hold plus its worst case is within the cap);
  3. `write_job_script` (with the job id in `AppCtx.jobs_admitting` from here);
  4. `Ledger.move_job(only_from=("reserved",), unless_cancel=True)` — a stop or cancel that set `cancel_requested` meanwhile wins and the job is never submitted (review B2);
  5. `_submit_script`; a `cancel_requested` that came while the submit was in flight cancels it at once.

  A refused submit releases the reservation (`refused`), quoting the scheduler. A submit whose answer never came — or that exited 0 printing no job id (review A8) — is `submit_unknown`: it keeps the reservation, the meter's sweep finds it by its name `hpcb-<job id>` in the accounting, a stop cancels it by that name (`scancel --name=` / `qselect -N | qdel`), and it is never submitted again. A `reserved` row no admission finished, that a WORKING accounting read never lists after `STALE_RESERVATION_S`, becomes `refused` — never a block's reservation (`pool_block`: it waits for its block however long it queues, review r2), and never on a read that failed.
- **The frozen job.sh** (`job_script`, `write_job_script(app, job_id, text, session_id=, deadline=)`): `<scratch>/jobs/<job id>/job.sh`, written once under noclobber (an existing one is refused, never replaced), and its sha256 read on the facility must equal the one meant.
  - **Header.** Built from the terms. Slurm: `--job-name=hpcb-<job id>`, nodes, time, `--deadline=<the cluster's local time>`, partition, account, `--exclusive` or `--ntasks-per-node=1 --cpus-per-task` (+`--mem`, `--gres`), `--array`, `--dependency`. The deadline is `DEADLINE_MARK` in the text; the writing call says it in the facility's local time (`date -d @N`, else BSD `date -r N`) and the hash is checked against the text with that value — sbatch reads `--deadline` in the submitting shell's time zone, and a TZ forced on sbatch would reach the job (probe, 2026-11-01). PBS: `-N`, `select`, walltime, `place=excl`, `-q`, `-A`, `-J`, `-W depend`.
  - **Body.** The session's `.cwd` and `.env` as they were at submit — copied beside the script (`session.cwd`, `session.env`, read-only) by the writing call (review A7: the job used to read them when it RAN) — then the command carried base64 (so no line of it is a directive), then `exit_code[.<task>]` written beside the script.
  - **Submission.** Slurm runs `sbatch --parsable --output=<dir>/slurm-%j.out` (`%A_%a` for arrays); PBS runs `qsub -o <dir>/pbs.out -j oe`. The receipt is read by `_receipts_in`.
- **The meter's tick** (`tick`): one login-shape call per tick over the facility's envelopes ([[meter]]). It updates every job row's `charged`, `committed`, `meter_source`, state and array counts, and each envelope's spent, committed and the unledgered jobs it counts (less `excluded` and the ids in `also_known`, jobs being adopted right now; on the envelope's accounts; one with no time limit to the deadline). An unread accounting keeps the last unledgered count (review B3) and does not set `metered_at`; an ended job it does not list keeps its last reading. A job still listed by squeue after the accounting says it ended is `running` (draining, review B6). It also applies the envelope's deadline (`at_deadline` `drain` or `abort`; an abort never cancels a job submitted unheld) and writes `meter_reading` rows and the `meter.tick` journal row.
- **`status` / `cancel` of `job-` and `env-` ids** answer in the one `BatchStatus` model (`kind`, `job`, `envelope`) and in `CancelResult`; both reconcile any open hand submit first. For a job, `abort` scancels or qdels it and `drain` cancels only its pending tasks. For an envelope, `drain` admits nothing more and `abort` cancels every job of it except one submitted unheld (named, with its own `cancel('job-…')`). `_scancel` cancels per id in one call and records, per id, what the scheduler answered (`Cancelled`); what the cancel DID is read from each job's state after it (`_cancel_words`): `cancelled: <ids>` only for a job now cancelled, "had already ended" for one that ended first, "cancel sent, still listed" for one on its way out, and what the scheduler refused — never scancel's exit code, which is 0 for a job that had already ended (probe r1; review B8). The stop's `after_stop` and an admission cancelled in flight decide the same way. A pending job a site limit holds (`site_limit`: `AssocGrp*`, `QOS*`, `*MaxJobs*`, a partition's or PBS server's limit) is said plainly in `status`: it will not start until that limit changes (round 2). A cancel says `draining` while one can still bill and `down` once none can.
- **D2** (`gate_envelope`, `gated_outcome`, `open_hand`, `hand_command`, `reconcile_hand`, `finish_hand`, `hand_results`, `reconcile_pending`). Round 3 (the user's decision) simplified it to what the design says: no wrapper, no hold, no mark file, no release-after-read-back, no cancel-while-held, no hand deadline — rounds 1–2 built those, and about half of all 2a findings came from them.
  - **Gate.** A recognised `sbatch`/`qsub` ANYWHERE on the line (`Submission.names`: `srun …; sbatch x` was named by its srun, review A4) while this session's envelope is open answers `needs_confirmation` and runs nothing. salloc and srun keep #176's advisory. `server._login_shell` refuses such a command, and `server._run_batch` refuses a template that submits jobs, pointing at `submit_job(array=…)` (review A6).
  - **The yes** (`server._run_held`): the command runs UNMODIFIED inside one remote shell (`hand_command`) that lists the user's jobs immediately before it (`<session dir>/hands/<token>/before`) and immediately after it ends (`…/after`, written by the shell itself — and from the EXIT trap, chained to the session's own, when the command calls `exit`), so the after-snapshot is taken when the command ENDS even if it outlives the sync-wait; the next call only reads it. The `hand` ledger row (`open_hand`: no login call) is written in the dispatch's locked step, before the command is sent. A command `exec` replaced: its outcome's arrival takes the after-snapshot then (`_late_after`).
  - **Adoption** (`reconcile_hand` on any outcome; the next status / submit_job / stop for a command still running): the jobs in after − before (`comm -13`), not already ledgered or excluded, have their request read (`_slurm_request`: ReqTRES / AllocTRES / TRES billing, the most of them, stepped arrays by their step; `_pbs_request`: `array_indices_submitted`; no walltime → the envelope's horizon), up to `READ_BATCH` (100) per call — the rest are adopted by the next call — after one meter tick (those ids `also_known`). Each is ledgered (source `adopt`, `unheld=1`, `deadline` none, reason "appeared while your approved command ran"), metered, and reserved after the fact even past the cap (`ignore_owner`): over it, the envelope turns `exhausted` (admission closes) and the notice says the approved submit put it over its cap, by how much. The notice names them as "jobs that appeared while your approved command ran", says no envelope deadline is applied, and points at `exclude_jobs` — which, on the user's yes, takes an adopted job off the count (`refused`). A job that cannot be read (ended and purged) is named and left to the meter's sweep. Nothing here ever cancels anything.
  - **Never cancelled by hpc-bridge on its own** (an `at_deadline` abort skips them): the stop asks about them by name; `jobs="cancel"`, `cancel('env-…', 'abort')` and `cancel('job-…', 'abort')` end them because the user chose.
  - **The hand closes** once its jobs are all adopted (or the command's dispatch failed, or no snapshot could be read — then the meter's sweep counts what it submitted), or after a day. Every read is bounded (`__hb_bound`); `reconcile_pending` stops after `RECONCILE_BUDGET_S`. About three login calls per hand submit (the command, one read, one tick), however many jobs it makes (review A8).
  - **`submit_job(adopt=<id>)`** brings an existing job under the envelope, at its full request and never below the queue's minimum. It is never cancelled for not fitting (it already runs); the user is asked instead.
- **The compute block** (`block_admission(app, shape, partition=, account=, sizing=)`, called by `_ensure_endpoint_up` with `confirm_spend` before its lock): while this session's envelope is open, a block it confirms is reserved as a `pool_block` row at the size THAT call confirms — its nodes, partition, account, the partition's billing (else billed cores) × max(walltime, the queue's minimum charge) (review B1). A confirmed block resized by a later confirmation is reserved again. If it doesn't fit, the answer is `needs_confirmation` naming the envelope. The reservation holds until the stop (`close_admission`). The block's own scheduler job, once known (`ShapeRuntime.block_job`), is linked to the reservation (`_link_block`); every other parsl pilot still counts as unledgered.
- **The stop** (`billing_jobs`, `stop_question`, `cancel_for_stop`, `keep_for_stop`, `after_stop`, `stop_without_channel`, `close_admission`, `ledger_unreadable`; called by `server._stop_endpoint`):
  - **One deadline** (`server.STOP_BUDGET_S`, 300 s, review r3): every phase — the reads, the adoption, the cancels (one scheduler call), the read after the release — takes from it; past it the answer is `draining` and says what it could not confirm (`unconfirmed`).
  - **A stop that asks changes nothing** (review r3): it only READS — `hand_results(adopt=False)` and `billing_jobs(reconcile=False)` — and asks when a job of ours can bill or an approved hand submit is unaccounted for (jobs that appeared, not yet adopted; a command still running), naming them (`hand_words`). Nothing is cancelled, released, adopted or closed.
  - **Admission closes FIRST once it proceeds** (`close_admission`, review r2, P5): a submit_job arriving while the stop releases the blocks (up to 180 s) is proposed afresh, never admitted from the old envelope, and an admission already in flight loses at `move_job(…, while_admitting=True)`. Then open hand submits are adopted (their notes kept for the answer), and the answer always reads the ledger again (`after_stop` → `billing_jobs`), so a job that got in anyway — or an approved command still running — keeps the stop from `down`.
  - **Which jobs.** Every job of ours that can still bill (any session's, a dead server's, a held one), on ANY facility (review B6): this facility's read fresh by the meter (open hand submits reconciled first) when a login channel is bound; every other's from the ledger, aged out by its deadline + walltime only on Slurm, which enforces the deadline — a PBS job carries none and stays until a read shows it ended (review r2, P5). Blocks are the stop's own.
  - **`jobs="cancel"`** cancels this facility's — an adopted one too, the user chose — in ONE scheduler call (a submit of unknown fate by name; an admission in flight asked to give up) and drains their envelope. Another facility's are not cancelled from here and stay listed. The answer is `draining` while one can still bill, then `down`.
  - **`jobs="keep"`** leaves them running under their envelope (now `draining`: status and cancel only) and their `--deadline`; an admission in flight gives up. The answer is `draining`, never `down`, and names them and their `cancel(…)`.
  - **With no endpoint bound**, nothing can be cancelled from here, and the answer says to connect first.
  - **An unreadable ledger** (`ledger_unreadable`): the blocks are released and the answer is never `down`.
  - **After any stop** the facility's envelopes admit nothing more.

- **The job's env** (block environments, PR 3; [[envs]]). `submit_job(…, env=)` takes the job's own env, else the session's.
  - **While it builds:** the answer is `busy` / `env_building` and nothing is submitted.
  - **A failed build** is `_refused`.
  - **The job script** (`job_script`, through `Spec.env_rec`) activates the env after the session's env line. A gone env exits 88 with the refusal on stderr.
  - **Where it shows:** the ledger's `job.env`, `JobInfo.env` (`job_info(j, app)`: its state as this server knows it, review S11) and `JobTerms.env`.
  - **What it says:** the job's own built env says what the session's would (`envs.built_notice`, S14; `submit_job` wraps `_submit_spec`, which holds the rest of the submit).
  - **A gated hand submit** (D2) runs unmodified, without the env.

> [!warning] The envelope binds only what hpc-bridge starts
> `terms.binds` says it and the envelope repeats it. A submit the lexer cannot see (Python's subprocess, an `sbatch` inside a script) passes the gate; the meter's sweep counts and names it. A gated hand submit's jobs carry no envelope deadline (the command runs as written): they are counted and adopted after the fact, which is the limit the design states. A stolen Compute token is not bound at all. The site backstop is "none" unless the user names one; on the fake cluster's `site` profile, `AccountingStorageEnforce=…,safe` makes Slurm hold a job that could pass its GrpTRESMins.
