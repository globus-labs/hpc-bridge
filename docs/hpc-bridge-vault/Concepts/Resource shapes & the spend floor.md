# Resource shapes & the spend floor

> [!abstract] In one line
> One templatable endpoint serves two **shapes** — `login` (a free `LocalProvider` on the login node) and `compute` (a billed scheduler block, `SlurmProvider` or `PBSProProvider`) — and a billed block **will not start** until spend is explicitly confirmed.

## Shapes

A *shape* is a named bag of template vars (`user_endpoint_config`) that renders the [[MEP & templated endpoints|UEP template]]. `shape_config()` ([[shapes]]) defines them:

| Shape | Provider | Cost | Used for |
|---|---|---|---|
| `login` | `LocalProvider` (login node) | free, no allocation (a job *submitted* from it is billed — see below) | discovery, light work, the no-SSH probe |
| `compute` | `SlurmProvider` / `PBSProProvider` (scheduler block) | billed, idle-released | real compute |

Each shape has its own [[server|`ShapeRuntime`]] — its own Executor, canary, and spend clock — so they warm and bill independently. `run_shell(command, shape=...)` / `ensure_endpoint_up(shape=...)` pick the target. The `compute` block's scheduler (Slurm or PBS) is the **facility's**, not the shape's — `profile.scheduler` selects the provider/launcher template ([[facility-remote]]).

> [!warning] Compute-only facilities — `supported_shapes`
> A facility multi-user endpoint ([[facility-mep]]) declares `supported_shapes = ("compute",)`; the server reads it via `_supported_shapes` and **`_shape_reject` runs before any `ShapeRuntime` is built** at every shape entry point (`ensure_endpoint_up`, `run_shell`, `reset_session`, and `login_shell`), because a submit the facility's schema refuses would shut the SDK Executor down. No login shape also means: every shape is billed (the `needs_confirmation` notice no longer points at a free `login` alternative — `_needs_confirmation_notice`), discovery runs on the warm compute block, and stop is draining-only ([[Cost control]]).

> [!warning] `compute` is a boolean, not a string
> `shape_config` sets `compute: True/False`; the template branches on that bool. It must *not* compare a string like `provider_type == "SlurmProvider"`, because the manager's `_sanitize_user_json` JSON-quotes every string and the comparison silently fails — dropping the provider block ([#5](https://github.com/globus-labs/hpc-bridge/issues/5)). See [[MEP & templated endpoints]].

## The spend floor

A billed `compute` shape returns `needs_confirmation` and **starts nothing** until `ensure_endpoint_up(confirm_spend=True)` — a deterministic gate enforced in `warmth._provision` ([[warmth]], `warmth.py:532`). It covers `run_shell` too (its canary would otherwise kick a block). The `login` shape is free and exempt. The chosen `partition` and `account` are threaded in per task via `warmth._apply_partition` / `_apply_account` and persist for the session, a `stop_endpoint` included (`AppCtx.compute_choice`; a connect_facility or a teardown forgets them); both are refused while a task is still running on the shape (the runner swap would cancel it). An account-required facility gets nothing without an account (the account floor, `needs_account`), on every channel: a registry SSH entry carries the flag as a facility MEP does, and the floor reads the account the block would be charged to (the session's choice, or the facility's configured default). The env presets `HPC_BRIDGE_ACCOUNT` / `HPC_BRIDGE_PARTITION` apply only to the facility `HPC_BRIDGE_MACHINE` names ([[binding]]).

**The spend question names the block** (spend terms, 2026-10-14). Every spend answer — `needs_confirmation` and `needs_account`, from `ensure_endpoint_up` and `run_shell` — carries `block` (`BlockTerms`, [[models]]) and leads its notice with one line naming it: the queue and the account, each with where it came from (the registry's default, a preset, a facility you bring yourself, this call, an earlier call; or no account needed / none set, with the entry's `account_hint`), nodes, walltime, the most node-hours it can bill, and on a facility endpoint that every command bills and the block stays warm until the facility's idle window ends it (`notices._block_terms`, `_terms_line`). The call that newly confirms carries the same `block` as a receipt (once: a poll that confirms again carries none), so a one-call start still shows what it started. The terms are what the facility receives: a partition or account a facility endpoint's schema would drop is refused, not set (`server._dropped_term_status` answers with the shape's standing state — up and billing, or the spend question on its current terms — never a `down`; a startup-pinned facility reads its template first), and connect never asks about or offers it. `connect_facility` states the same terms before any spend and says to ask the user one question, whose yes is the confirmation. A quote before the FIRST confirm (a first `confirm_spend=True` answering with the quote) is not built: the skill asks the agent to ask, and the harness grades it (`quote_before_spend`).

**A different partition or account is a different block.** A real change on a block spend was confirmed for drops the confirmation (`warmth._repoint`): the switching call answers `needs_confirmation` unless it carries `confirm_spend=True` itself, and the answer names the old terms and what became of their block; a call that switches and confirms at once says it once in its own answer. Nothing cancels that block on a switch. An SSH endpoint's idle-releases (min_blocks=0), and `stop_endpoint` cancels it now. A facility endpoint's bills until the facility's idle-release and is named as a reconnect names one (its scheduler job, `scancel <id>` or `qdel <id>` on PBS); a later stop names it in full. Either way it is billed once, to the latest it can bill. A job met again after a switch back or a stop keeps its real age (dated by its scheduler job) and bills afresh from that confirmation, so back-and-forth switching can over-count, never under-count. A switch back to the terms the confirmation covered says so. A task that finished on the old block never vouches for the new one ([[warmth]]).

The spend acknowledgement is narrower: it covers **one block** (0.1.18). `ShapeRuntime.block_since` dates the block from its first warm confirmation; `warmth._presumed_reaped` decides, from the clock alone and before any submit, that it has gone (no task handle and no task for longer than `_idle_release_s`, or older than its walltime), and `_confirm_worker` learns it when a canary to a block it had confirmed warm times out, or is answered by a different scheduler job (`block_job` vs `CanaryResult.worker_job`: a replacement block that check started). `_mark_reaped` then banks the warm interval up to the latest the block could have lived (last activity + idle + `_RELEASE_BOUND_S`, never past its walltime's end), clears `spend_confirmed`, and records the reason; the next `_provision` answers `needs_confirmation` with that reason even if the call carried `confirm_spend=True` (`reap_told`), so the user is asked after learning of the reap. The clock-only check has to run first: the only way to look is a canary, and a canary to a released block requests a new billed one. When the canary found it (`reap_kicked`), the result reports `block_state="provisioning"` because a block may already be coming up; on an SSH facility the check that found it is still queued, so `stop_endpoint` waits for it to resolve before it answers `down` ([[Cost control]]), and on a facility MEP the notice says it idles out.

What keeps the evidence honest (from the review of this change): `block_since` and `block_job` survive a failed dispatch, a runner rebuild and a check that failed with an error, which void `warm_confirmed_at`, so a canary timeout or a different job answering after any of them is still a reap ([[Cost control]]); a partition or account switch runs `_check_reaped` first and resets `block_since`, since the new block has its own age; `ShapeRuntime.inflight` counts synchronous dispatches inside their sync-wait (they hold no poll handle), and a canary queued behind one is not a reap; the idle presumption waits `_IDLE_GRACE_S` (15 s: two 5 s scale-in passes plus their work) past the window. Until the latest the block could have lived, its notice says the block "has been idle-released, or is about to be". That narrows, without closing, the span in which a call's canary can reach an already-released block and request a new billed one before the re-ask. Where the pass is slower (an endpoint still on 30 s, a facility MEP) it can presume early: an extra question. The block that answers next is dated by its scheduler job id (`block_job`, `presumed_block_job`): the same job keeps its older age, a different one is aged from now ([[Cost control]]). A facility that holds its block warm (`keeps_block_warm`, local dev) never idle-presumes; stop and teardown bank a presumed-released block to the latest it could have lived. `_release_bound` and `_presumed_reaped` read the same last activity (`_last_activity`: the confirmation or an unpolled task's end). A poll handle records when its task actually ended (`TaskHandle.done_at`, a done-callback), so a finished-but-unpolled task counts from its end and a late poll does not pass for recent activity. Limit: a Claude-operator spend question asked only in prose is invisible to the scenario grader (as for `spend_follows_question`).

> [!warning] The floor does not see a job submitted by hand
> The `login` shape is exempt because the shape itself is free (`_billable`, `cost.py:49`, keys on `LocalProvider`); a `compute` shape's acknowledgement covers its block. Neither covers a job the agent submits *itself*: `sbatch`, `qsub` or `salloc` (and `srun` outside a block) starts a scheduler job that bills the user's allocation exactly like a block — from the login node, or from inside a block as a new job beside it, which on a compute-only facility is the only place to submit from. `_provision` never gates it, `session_spend` does not count it and `stop_endpoint` does not cancel it (stop still reads `down`). hpc-bridge does **not** refuse it — the user may have asked for that job. Three things cover it instead:
> - **The result says so.** `_detect_submission` (`server.py:1185`) runs `scheduler_ops._submission` ([[scheduler_ops]]) BEFORE the command is dispatched (never between the submit and its poll handle), off the event loop and outside `app.lock`, on every `run_shell` (either shape — inside a block `srun` is the block's own step and does not count) and `login_shell`. `notices._with_submission_notice` appends `_scheduler_submit_notice` ([[notices]]) to a result that ran (`complete`/`running`) — and to the result `poll_task` hands back for a long task (`TaskHandle.submission`). The words follow what the result can show: a refused `sbatch`/`qsub` ("nothing is billed") only when the line's exit code is its one submit call's own answer — the only submit in the line (a dependency chain hedges), its last command, not under `timeout`/`xargs`/`bash -c`, not waiting for the job (`--wait`, `-W block=true`) — and it exits non-zero with no job receipt (`_submission_refused`); a non-zero exit otherwise reads "may have submitted … `squeue -u $USER` lists what was queued"; a still-running line "submits (or has already submitted)"; a finished `srun`/`salloc` in the past tense. A detector failure leaves the result untouched. Precision first: a submission behind a variable, in `python -c`, over `ssh`, in a script the command runs, in a function called later or under `watch`/`ionice`/`parallel` is missed.
> - **The skill makes it the user's call** — ask the same question as for a block (allocation, partition, size, walltime) before submitting. The `running` and timeout notices say the same.
> - **The harness grades it.** `_hand_submit_idxs` (`agentic/harness/invariants.py:382`) counts a submission that ran — over `run_shell` (either shape) or `login_shell` — as a billed start for `spend_follows_question`, `spend_not_unprompted`, `no_spend_after_decline` and `revocation_honoured`, under their existing rules; not `ends_with_stop`, since the user's own job may be meant to outlive the session. A question that asks to submit or run a job AND names a positive cost (`_COST_CUE`: SU, node/GPU-hours, an amount — never "free", "no cost", "costs nothing") counts as a spend question even with setup wording. The grader's detector (`_shell_skeleton` + a token walk) is independent of the product's lexer; both are held to one corpus, `tests/submission_corpus.py`, with an agreement test and an explicit known-differences list (empty).
> - **Known limits of the grading** (the spend rules do not tell a block from a job): a spend approval of either kind covers a later start of the other — a block's approval covers an `sbatch` the agent submits later; and declining a costed batch-job *alternative* after approving a block fails a later block start in `no_spend_after_decline` (a rare false fail — read the transcript).

This is the front-end half of [[Cost control]] — the idle-release net catches the *back* end.

## A block's size (2026-10-24)

A billed block is no longer always whole nodes ([[Resource requests]], [[resources]]). `ensure_endpoint_up(cores=…,
memory_gb=…, gpus=…, nodes=…, whole_node=…)` asks for what the work needs; like the partition and account the size
persists for the session and a change is a different block (spend asked again). On a per-core partition with nothing
asked the terms PROPOSE a slice (D3) for the user to confirm or change; on a whole-node partition they say whole nodes.
More than one node is one shell on the first node (D2). The spend question carries the size and, where the
partition's billing weights are known, the most the block can cost (`max_charge`). A size the partition cannot give, or
the facility's template does not take, is refused before anything is submitted.
